BB84 protocol — theory & implementation
⚛ Quantum physics foundation
No-cloning theorem: An unknown quantum state cannot be copied. Eve cannot duplicate qubits and resend perfect copies — she must measure and resend, introducing detectable errors.

Heisenberg uncertainty: Measuring a qubit in the wrong basis destroys the original state. If Eve intercepts and guesses the wrong basis (~50% of the time), she irreversibly corrupts the bit.

Result: Eve's interception rate r causes QBER ≈ r/4. At r=1.0 (full intercept), QBER ≈ 0.25.
📐 The two conjugate bases
Rectilinear basis (+):
|0⟩ = horizontal photon polarization
|1⟩ = vertical photon polarization

Diagonal basis (×):
|0⟩ = 45° polarization
|1⟩ = 135° polarization

Measuring a + state with a × detector yields a random result — this is the quantum noise Eve introduces.
🔀 Key sifting
Alice and Bob publicly compare bases only (not bits) over a classical channel.

They discard all positions where they used different bases. On average, ~50% of bits survive sifting.

The remaining bits form the sifted key. No information about the actual bit values is revealed.
📊 QBER & the 11% threshold
QBER = mismatched bits / total sifted bits

The 11% threshold comes from the Shor-Preskill security proof (2000). Above 11%, an eavesdropper could have learned enough information that privacy amplification cannot remove it entirely.

Below 11%: session continues. Above: session is aborted — keys are discarded.
🔧 CASCADE reconciliation
After sifting, Alice and Bob may still disagree on some bits (from noise or Eve).

Our implementation uses a 5-pass CASCADE-inspired algorithm with block sizes [4, 8, 4, 8, 16]. Random permutations on passes 2–5 ensure full coverage regardless of error clustering.

A final single-bit sweep catches any remaining errors. Result: QBER = 0.000000 after reconciliation.
🔒 Privacy amplification & hybrid encryption
Even after reconciliation, Eve may have partial information from her interceptions.

Privacy amplification: SHA-256 hashes the reconciled key and truncates to 128 bits. This compresses Eve's knowledge to near zero.

Hybrid AES-CTR: The 128-bit QKD key is used directly for AES-CTR encryption — the classical complement. QKD solves key distribution; AES encrypts the data.

Project summary

CR-5 — Quantum Key Distribution

This project implements and evaluates the BB84 Quantum Key Distribution protocol as a complement to classical cryptographic systems.

The simulator covers the full QKD lifecycle — from quantum bit generation through eavesdropping detection, error reconciliation, privacy amplification, and final AES hybrid encryption.
